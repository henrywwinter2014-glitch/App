    How the simulation works

    Each trading day, every price moves according to a factor model: the whole market, its sector, interest rates, currency and region, plus company-specific noise. Prices start near recent real-world levels and then evolve on their own; they are not live quotes.

    Dividend payers go ex-dividend on a fixed schedule (monthly, quarterly, twice a year or yearly). The share price drops by the dividend on the ex-date, and anyone holding shares that day is paid about two weeks later (four weeks for UK shares). Companies review their dividends once a year: aristocrats almost always raise theirs, while very high yields are more likely to be cut.

    Companies report earnings each quarter, which can move the price sharply. Expensive stocks may split; penny stocks below 50 cents may reverse split. Leveraged and inverse ETFs reset daily, so they drift away from their multiple over time. London shares are priced in pounds and converted to dollars at a moving GBP/USD rate.
